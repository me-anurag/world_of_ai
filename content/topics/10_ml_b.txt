# FIELD 5 — Machine Learning, part B: unsupervised, evaluation, settings, AutoML, interpretation, theory
!defaults d=int st=foundational

######## L2: Unsupervised learning
== ml.unsup | Unsupervised learning methods
def: Algorithms that find structure without labels: clustering, dimensionality reduction, density estimation, anomaly detection, pattern mining.
pre: ml.paradigms.unsupervised

#### Clustering
== ml.unsup.clustering | Clustering
def: Group similar items together. Choice of algorithm depends on cluster shape, size, noise and whether you know how many clusters there are.
pre: found.linalg.geom.distance
d: beg

== ml.unsup.kmeans | k-means
def: Alternate between assigning points to the nearest centroid and moving centroids to the mean of their points. Fast; assumes round, similar-sized clusters. k-means++ chooses good starting centroids.
f: min Σᵢ ‖xᵢ − μ_c(i)‖²
pre: ml.unsup.clustering, found.opt.other.em
paper: Lloyd | Least Squares Quantization in PCM | 1982
d: beg

== ml.unsup.hierarchical | Hierarchical clustering
def: Merge (agglomerative) or split clusters step by step, producing a dendrogram; linkage (single, complete, average, Ward) decides cluster distance.
pre: ml.unsup.clustering, found.discrete.graphs.trees
d: beg

== ml.unsup.dbscan | Density-based clustering (DBSCAN, HDBSCAN, OPTICS)
def: Clusters are dense regions separated by sparse ones; finds arbitrary shapes and labels outliers as noise, without fixing the number of clusters.
pre: ml.unsup.clustering
paper: Ester, Kriegel, Sander, Xu | A Density-Based Algorithm for Discovering Clusters in Large Spatial Databases with Noise | 1996

== ml.unsup.meanshift | Mean shift & affinity propagation
def: Mean shift moves points uphill on a density estimate to its modes; affinity propagation passes messages to choose exemplars.
pre: ml.unsup.clustering, found.stats.est.kde
d: adv

== ml.unsup.spectral | Spectral clustering
def: Embed points using eigenvectors of a similarity graph's Laplacian, then cluster in that space. Finds non-convex clusters.
pre: ml.unsup.kmeans, found.discrete.graphs.adjacency
paper: Ng, Jordan, Weiss | On Spectral Clustering: Analysis and an Algorithm | 2001
x: dom.graph.spectral
d: adv

== ml.unsup.gmm | Gaussian mixture model
def: Model data as a mix of Gaussians fitted by EM. A soft, probabilistic version of k-means that also gives a density.
pre: ml.unsup.kmeans, found.prob.dist.mixture, prob.inference.em

== ml.unsup.cluster-eval | Cluster evaluation (silhouette, ARI, elbow)
def: Judge clusterings without labels (silhouette, Davies–Bouldin, elbow plots) or against known labels (adjusted Rand index, mutual information).
pre: ml.unsup.clustering

#### Dimensionality reduction
== ml.unsup.dimred | Dimensionality reduction
def: Represent high-dimensional data with fewer dimensions, for visualization, compression, denoising or as a preprocessing step.
pre: ml.concepts.curse
d: beg

== ml.unsup.pca | PCA
def: Find the orthogonal directions of greatest variance (eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, or via SVD) and project onto the top few.
pre: ml.unsup.dimred, found.linalg.decomp.svd, found.prob.rv.cov-matrix
paper: Pearson | On Lines and Planes of Closest Fit to Systems of Points in Space | 1901

== ml.unsup.ica | Independent component analysis
def: Separate mixed signals into statistically independent sources (the cocktail-party problem).
pre: ml.unsup.pca, found.info.mi
paper: Hyvärinen, Oja | Independent Component Analysis: Algorithms and Applications | 2000
d: adv

== ml.unsup.nmf | Non-negative matrix factorization & dictionary learning
def: Factor data into non-negative (parts-based) or sparse components, giving interpretable building blocks for images and text.
pre: ml.unsup.pca, found.linalg.decomp.lowrank
paper: Lee, Seung | Learning the Parts of Objects by Non-negative Matrix Factorization | 1999
x: safe.interp
d: adv

== ml.unsup.manifold | Manifold learning (MDS, Isomap, LLE, Laplacian eigenmaps)
def: Assume data lies on a curved low-dimensional surface and unfold it while preserving distances or neighbourhoods.
pre: ml.unsup.pca, found.discrete.graphs.adjacency
paper: Tenenbaum, de Silva, Langford | A Global Geometric Framework for Nonlinear Dimensionality Reduction | 2000
d: adv

== ml.unsup.tsne | t-SNE
def: Nonlinear embedding into 2D/3D that preserves local neighbourhoods, for visualizing clusters. Distances between clusters in the plot are not meaningful.
pre: ml.unsup.manifold, found.info.kl, found.prob.dist.student-t
paper: van der Maaten, Hinton | Visualizing Data using t-SNE | 2008

== ml.unsup.umap | UMAP
def: Faster neighbourhood-preserving embedding than t-SNE that keeps more global structure; the default for visualizing embeddings.
pre: ml.unsup.tsne
paper: McInnes, Healy, Melville | UMAP: Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection for Dimension Reduction | 2018
st: dominant

== ml.unsup.random-proj | Random projections (Johnson–Lindenstrauss)
def: Multiplying by a random matrix approximately preserves pairwise distances in far fewer dimensions.
pre: ml.unsup.dimred, found.prob.limits.concentration
d: adv

#### Density, anomalies, patterns
== ml.unsup.anomaly | Anomaly & outlier detection
def: Find rare, unusual points (fraud, faults, intrusions): isolation forest, one-class SVM, local outlier factor, density and reconstruction-error methods.
paper: Liu, Ting, Zhou | Isolation Forest | 2008
to: dom.anomaly

== ml.unsup.density | Density estimation
def: Estimate p(x) from data: histograms, kernel density estimation, mixtures, and deep generative models.
pre: found.stats.est.kde
to: dl.gen

== ml.unsup.assoc | Association rule mining (Apriori)
def: Find items that frequently occur together ("people who buy X buy Y") with support and confidence thresholds. Classic data mining.
pre: found.discrete.combinatorics
paper: Agrawal, Srikant | Fast Algorithms for Mining Association Rules | 1994
st: classic
d: beg

######## L2: Evaluation
== ml.eval | Model evaluation
def: Measuring how well a model will do on new data, and comparing models fairly. Choosing the wrong metric or split silently misleads.
pre: ml.concepts.generalization
d: beg

== ml.eval.cv | Cross-validation
def: Rotate which part of the data is held out (k-fold, stratified, leave-one-out, grouped, time-series splits) to get a more reliable estimate with limited data.
paper: Stone | Cross-Validatory Choice and Assessment of Statistical Predictions | 1974
d: beg

== ml.eval.cls-metrics | Classification metrics (confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1)
def: Accuracy misleads on imbalanced data. Precision: of predicted positives, how many were right. Recall: of actual positives, how many were found. F1 balances them.
f: P = TP/(TP+FP);  R = TP/(TP+FN);  F1 = 2PR/(P+R)
d: beg

== ml.eval.roc | ROC & precision–recall curves, AUC
def: Sweep the decision threshold to trade true positives against false positives (ROC) or precision against recall (PR). AUC summarizes the whole curve.
pre: ml.eval.cls-metrics, found.stats.decision.threshold

== ml.eval.reg-metrics | Regression metrics (MSE, RMSE, MAE, R²)
def: Squared error punishes large mistakes; absolute error is robust; R² compares against always predicting the mean.
d: beg

== ml.eval.calibration | Probability calibration
def: A calibrated model's "70%" happens 70% of the time. Check with reliability diagrams and expected calibration error; fix with Platt scaling, isotonic regression or temperature scaling.
pre: ml.eval.cls-metrics, found.prob.rv.pmf
paper: Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger | On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks | 2017
x: prob.bayesml.uncertainty

== ml.eval.curves | Learning & validation curves
def: Plot performance against training-set size or a hyperparameter to see whether more data or more capacity would help.
pre: ml.concepts.bias-variance
d: beg

== ml.eval.baselines | Baselines & ablations
def: Compare against trivial baselines (predict the majority class) and remove components one at a time (ablation) to see what actually helps.
d: beg

== ml.eval.selection | Model selection
def: Choosing among models or hyperparameters using validation scores, cross-validation or information criteria (AIC, BIC) without touching the test set.
pre: ml.eval.cv
paper: Akaike | A New Look at the Statistical Model Identification | 1974

== ml.eval.hpo | Hyperparameter optimization
def: Search hyperparameters by grid search, random search (often better), successive halving/Hyperband or Bayesian optimization.
pre: ml.eval.selection
paper: Bergstra, Bengio | Random Search for Hyper-Parameter Optimization | 2012
x: prob.bayesopt

== ml.eval.comparison | Statistical comparison of models
def: Is model A really better? Use confidence intervals, paired tests across folds or examples, and enough test data. Benchmark gains are often within noise.
pre: ml.eval.cv, found.stats.testing.hypothesis
d: adv

######## L2: Learning settings
== ml.settings | Learning settings beyond i.i.d. supervised learning
def: Variants of the standard setup: learning across tasks, from little data, over time, across devices, or with the learner choosing what to label.
pre: ml.concepts.generalization

== ml.settings.transfer | Transfer learning & fine-tuning
def: Reuse a model trained on one task or dataset as the starting point for another. The default way to use pretrained models.
to: llm.post.sft, dl.repr
st: dominant
paper: Pan, Yang | A Survey on Transfer Learning | 2010

== ml.settings.domain | Domain adaptation & generalization
def: Make a model trained on one distribution work on a shifted one (synthetic → real, hospital A → hospital B).
pre: ml.settings.transfer, ml.concepts.shift
st: active
d: adv

== ml.settings.multitask | Multitask learning
def: Train one model on several related tasks with shared representations, so each task helps the others.
paper: Caruana | Multitask Learning | 1997

== ml.settings.fewshot | Few-shot & meta-learning
def: Learn new tasks from a handful of examples by "learning to learn" across many tasks (prototypical networks, MAML). In-context learning in LLMs achieves this without weight updates.
pre: ml.settings.multitask
paper: Finn, Abbeel, Levine | Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning for Fast Adaptation of Deep Networks | 2017
to: sys.prompting.icl
st: active
d: adv

== ml.settings.zeroshot | Zero-shot learning
def: Recognize classes never seen in training using side information (attributes, text descriptions); CLIP and LLMs made this routine.
pre: ml.settings.fewshot
to: dom.mm.clip
d: adv

== ml.settings.continual | Continual (lifelong) learning
def: Learn a sequence of tasks without forgetting earlier ones. Neural nets suffer catastrophic forgetting; fixes include replay, regularization (EWC) and modular parameters.
pre: ml.paradigms.online
paper: Kirkpatrick et al. | Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks | 2017
st: active
d: adv

== ml.settings.federated | Federated learning
def: Train across many devices or organizations while data stays local; only model updates are shared and averaged (FedAvg).
paper: McMahan, Moore, Ramage, Hampson, Agüera y Arcas | Communication-Efficient Learning of Deep Networks from Decentralized Data | 2017
to: safe.privacy
d: adv

== ml.settings.ltr | Learning to rank
def: Learn to order items by relevance (pointwise, pairwise, listwise losses). Powers search engines and recommenders.
pre: ml.paradigms.tasks
paper: Burges | From RankNet to LambdaRank to LambdaMART: An Overview | 2010
to: dom.ir

== ml.settings.noisy | Learning with noisy labels & PU learning
def: Robust training when some labels are wrong, or when only positive and unlabelled examples exist.
pre: ml.paradigms.weak
d: adv

== ml.settings.curriculum | Curriculum learning
def: Present training examples from easy to hard, as in human teaching.
paper: Bengio, Louradour, Collobert, Weston | Curriculum Learning | 2009
d: adv

== ml.active | Active learning
def: The model chooses which unlabelled examples to send for labelling (most uncertain, most informative), cutting labelling cost.
pre: ml.paradigms.semisup, prob.decision.voi
paper: Settles | Active Learning Literature Survey | 2009

######## L2: AutoML
== ml.automl | AutoML
def: Automating the ML pipeline: preprocessing, model choice, hyperparameters and architecture.
pre: ml.eval.hpo
paper: Hutter, Kotthoff, Vanschoren | Automated Machine Learning: Methods, Systems, Challenges | 2019

== ml.automl.nas | Neural architecture search
def: Search over network architectures with RL, evolution or differentiable relaxations (DARTS). Produced EfficientNet; less central since Transformers standardized architectures.
pre: evo.neuro
paper: Zoph, Le | Neural Architecture Search with Reinforcement Learning | 2017
d: adv

== ml.automl.meta | Meta-learning for AutoML & warm starting
def: Use results from past datasets to pick good starting configurations for new ones.
pre: ml.settings.fewshot
d: adv

######## L2: Interpreting models
== ml.interp | Interpreting & explaining models
def: Understanding why a model predicts what it does: global feature importance, local explanations, and inherently interpretable models.
pre: ml.sup.trees, ml.eval
to: safe.interp

== ml.interp.importance | Feature importance (impurity, permutation)
def: How much each feature matters: impurity reduction in trees (biased), or the score drop when a feature is shuffled (permutation importance).
pre: ml.ensemble.rf
d: beg

== ml.interp.pdp | Partial dependence & ICE plots
def: Show how predictions change as one feature varies, on average (PDP) or per example (ICE).

== ml.interp.shap | SHAP & Shapley values
def: Attribute a prediction to features fairly using Shapley values from cooperative game theory. The most used local explanation method for tabular models.
pre: ml.ensemble.gbm
paper: Lundberg, Lee | A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions | 2017
x: rl.multiagent

== ml.interp.lime | LIME & local surrogates
def: Explain one prediction by fitting a simple model to the black box's behaviour around that input.
paper: Ribeiro, Singh, Guestrin | "Why Should I Trust You?": Explaining the Predictions of Any Classifier | 2016

== ml.interp.counterfactual | Counterfactual explanations
def: The smallest change to the input that would flip the decision ("approved if income were $5k higher").
x: prob.causal.counterfactual
d: adv

######## L2: Learning theory
== ml.theory | Learning theory
def: Mathematical answers to: when can a model learn from finite data, how much data is enough, and why does it generalize?
pre: ml.concepts.erm, found.prob.limits.concentration
paper: Shalev-Shwartz, Ben-David | Understanding Machine Learning: From Theory to Algorithms | 2014
d: adv

== ml.theory.pac | PAC learning
def: Probably Approximately Correct: a concept class is learnable if, with enough samples, a learner returns a hypothesis with error ≤ ε with probability ≥ 1 − δ.
paper: Valiant | A Theory of the Learnable | 1984
d: adv

== ml.theory.vc | VC dimension
def: The largest set of points a hypothesis class can label in every possible way. It bounds how much data is needed to generalize.
pre: ml.theory.pac
paper: Vapnik, Chervonenkis | On the Uniform Convergence of Relative Frequencies of Events to Their Probabilities | 1971
d: adv

== ml.theory.bounds | Generalization bounds (uniform convergence, Rademacher, stability)
def: Bounds on test error in terms of training error, sample size and model complexity, measured by VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, margins or algorithmic stability.
pre: ml.theory.vc
d: adv

== ml.theory.nfl | No free lunch & the need for priors
def: Averaged over all possible problems, no learner beats any other. Learning works only because real problems have structure that inductive biases exploit.
pre: ml.concepts.inductive-bias
paper: Wolpert | The Lack of A Priori Distinctions Between Learning Algorithms | 1996
x: evo.core.theory
d: adv

== ml.theory.regret | Online learning & regret
def: For adversarial data streams, compare cumulative loss to the best fixed expert in hindsight; algorithms like multiplicative weights achieve sublinear regret.
pre: ml.paradigms.online
x: rl.bandits
d: adv

== ml.theory.deep | Theory of deep learning (double descent, implicit bias, NTK)
def: Why do huge overparameterized networks generalize? Double descent (test error falls again past the interpolation point), SGD's implicit regularization, and the neural tangent kernel regime.
pre: ml.theory.bounds, ml.concepts.bias-variance
paper: Belkin, Hsu, Ma, Mandal | Reconciling Modern Machine-Learning Practice and the Classical Bias–Variance Trade-off | 2019
to: dl
st: active
d: adv
