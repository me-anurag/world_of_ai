# FIELD 9 — Large Language Models, part B: efficiency, inference, decoding, long context, evaluation, behaviour, model families
!defaults d=int st=foundational

######## L2: Efficiency & adaptation
== llm.eff | Efficiency & adaptation
def: Making LLMs cheaper to fine-tune, store and run: fewer trainable parameters, fewer bits per weight, smaller models.
pre: llm.basics.params

== llm.eff.peft | Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)
def: Train a tiny number of new parameters while freezing the model: adapters, prefix/prompt tuning, LoRA. Lets one base model serve many tasks.
pre: dl.train.finetune
paper: Houlsby et al. | Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning for NLP | 2019

== llm.eff.lora | LoRA
def: Learn a low-rank update ΔW = BA for chosen weight matrices; cheap to train, small to store, mergeable into the weights afterwards.
f: W' = W + BA,  rank r ≪ d
pre: llm.eff.peft, found.linalg.decomp.lowrank
paper: Hu et al. | LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models | 2021
st: dominant

== llm.eff.qlora | QLoRA
def: LoRA on top of a 4-bit quantized base model, so large models can be fine-tuned on a single GPU.
pre: llm.eff.lora, llm.eff.quant
paper: Dettmers, Pagnoni, Holtzman, Zettlemoyer | QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs | 2023
st: dominant

== llm.eff.quant | Quantization
def: Store and compute weights (and sometimes activations and KV cache) in 8, 4 or fewer bits. Post-training methods (GPTQ, AWQ) keep accuracy close; formats like GGUF run models on laptops.
pre: found.numerics.formats
paper: Frantar, Ashkboos, Hoefler, Alistarh | GPTQ: Accurate Post-Training Quantization for Generative Pre-trained Transformers | 2022
st: dominant

== llm.eff.distill | Knowledge distillation
def: Train a small student to match a large teacher's output distributions (soft labels), transferring much of its ability.
pre: found.info.kl
paper: Hinton, Vinyals, Dean | Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network | 2015
st: dominant

== llm.eff.pruning | Pruning & sparsity
def: Remove unimportant weights, heads or layers (unstructured or structured) to shrink models; structured pruning gives real speedups.
paper: Frankle, Carbin | The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis: Finding Sparse, Trainable Neural Networks | 2018
d: adv

== llm.eff.slm | Small language models
def: Models of roughly 1–15B parameters trained on curated and synthetic data, good enough for many tasks and able to run on-device.
pre: llm.eff.distill, llm.pretrain.synthetic
st: active

######## L2: Inference & serving
== llm.inference | Inference & serving
def: Running LLMs fast and cheaply: memory management, batching and tricks that cut the cost of generating each token.
pre: llm.basics.params, found.cs.prog.systems

== llm.inference.phases | Prefill vs decode; latency & throughput
def: Prefill processes the prompt in parallel (compute-bound); decode generates one token at a time (memory-bound). Key metrics: time to first token, tokens per second, cost per million tokens.

== llm.inference.kvcache | KV cache
def: Store each layer's keys and values for past tokens so each new token only computes its own. Its memory grows with context length and batch size, and dominates serving cost.
pre: llm.inference.phases, dl.arch.attention.qkv

== llm.inference.batching | Continuous batching & PagedAttention
def: Add and remove requests from a running batch at every step, and store the KV cache in pages like virtual memory (vLLM) to serve many users efficiently.
pre: llm.inference.kvcache
paper: Kwon et al. | Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention | 2023
st: dominant
d: adv

== llm.inference.prefix-cache | Prompt (prefix) caching
def: Reuse the KV cache for repeated prompt prefixes (system prompts, documents, tools), cutting cost and latency for agents and RAG.
pre: llm.inference.kvcache
st: dominant

== llm.inference.speculative | Speculative decoding
def: A small draft model proposes several tokens and the large model verifies them in one pass, producing identical outputs faster.
pre: llm.inference.phases
paper: Leviathan, Kalman, Matias | Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding | 2022
d: adv

######## L2: Decoding
== llm.decode | Decoding strategies
def: How the next token is picked from the predicted distribution. Trades determinism, diversity and quality.
pre: llm.basics.context, found.prob.dist.categorical

== llm.decode.greedy | Greedy & beam search
def: Always take the most likely token (greedy) or keep the best few partial sequences (beam). Good for short deterministic outputs; repetitive in open-ended text.
pre: sym.search.informed.beam
d: beg

== llm.decode.temperature | Temperature
def: Divide logits by T before softmax: T < 1 sharpens toward the top token, T > 1 flattens toward randomness.
f: pᵢ ∝ exp(zᵢ / T)
d: beg

== llm.decode.topk-topp | Top-k, top-p (nucleus) & min-p sampling
def: Sample only from the k most likely tokens, the smallest set whose probability sums to p, or tokens above a fraction of the top probability. Avoids the incoherent tail.
pre: llm.decode.temperature
paper: Holtzman, Buys, Du, Forbes, Choi | The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration | 2019
d: beg

== llm.decode.penalties | Repetition & frequency penalties, stop sequences
def: Down-weight tokens already used and stop generation at chosen strings.
d: beg

== llm.decode.constrained | Constrained decoding (JSON schema, grammars)
def: Mask out tokens that would violate a schema, regular expression or grammar at each step, guaranteeing valid structured output for tools and programs.
pre: found.cs.theory.automata
to: sys.prompting.structured
st: dominant

######## L2: Long context
== llm.longctx | Long context
def: Making models read hundreds of thousands to millions of tokens. Needs position schemes that extrapolate, efficient attention and enough training on long documents.
pre: llm.basics.context, dl.arch.transformer.rope

== llm.longctx.extension | Context extension (position interpolation, YaRN)
def: Rescale rotary positions so a model trained on short contexts works on longer ones after brief fine-tuning.
paper: Peng, Quesnelle, Fan, Shippole | YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension of Large Language Models | 2023
d: adv

== llm.longctx.usage | How well long context is used (lost in the middle)
def: Models often use information at the start and end of the context better than the middle; needle-in-a-haystack tests are easy, multi-step use of long context is harder.
paper: Liu et al. | Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts | 2023
to: sys.context
st: active

######## L2: Evaluation
== llm.eval | LLM evaluation
def: Measuring what models can do: static benchmarks, human and model-judged comparisons, and task-specific evals. Benchmarks saturate and leak, so evaluation keeps moving.
pre: llm.basics, ml.eval

== llm.eval.benchmarks | Knowledge & reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, GPQA, MATH, HLE)
def: Multiple-choice knowledge (MMLU), graduate-level science (GPQA), competition math (MATH, AIME) and very hard expert questions (Humanity's Last Exam).
paper: Hendrycks et al. | Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding | 2020

== llm.eval.code | Code & agent benchmarks (HumanEval, SWE-bench)
def: Generate functions that pass unit tests (HumanEval, pass@k) or resolve real GitHub issues in full repositories (SWE-bench).
pre: dom.code
paper: Jimenez et al. | SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues? | 2023

== llm.eval.arena | Human preference arenas
def: Users compare anonymous model answers side by side and an Elo/Bradley–Terry rating ranks models (Chatbot Arena / LMArena).
paper: Chiang et al. | Chatbot Arena: An Open Platform for Evaluating LLMs by Human Preference | 2024

== llm.eval.judge | LLM-as-a-judge
def: Use a strong model to grade outputs against a rubric. Scales evaluation, but judges have biases (position, length, self-preference).
paper: Zheng et al. | Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena | 2023
to: sys.eval

== llm.eval.contamination | Contamination & benchmark validity
def: Test questions leaking into training data inflate scores; mitigations are held-out, fresh or private test sets.
pre: ml.concepts.leakage
d: adv

######## L2: Behaviour & failure modes
== llm.behavior | Behaviour & failure modes
def: Characteristic ways LLMs fail, which every application has to design around.
pre: llm.basics

== llm.behavior.hallucination | Hallucination
def: Fluent, confident statements that are false or unsupported. Reduced by retrieval, citations, calibration training and abstaining; not eliminated.
pre: llm.basics.knowledge
paper: Ji et al. | Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation | 2023
to: sys.rag

== llm.behavior.sycophancy | Sycophancy
def: Telling users what they want to hear, agreeing with mistaken claims, a side effect of optimizing for human approval.
pre: llm.post.rlhf
paper: Sharma et al. | Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models | 2023
to: safe.alignment

== llm.behavior.sensitivity | Prompt sensitivity & position biases
def: Small wording or ordering changes can change answers; models favour certain positions and formats.

== llm.behavior.icl-mechanism | How in-context learning works (induction heads)
def: Attention circuits that find earlier occurrences of the current token and copy what followed explain much of in-context learning.
pre: dl.arch.transformer.block
paper: Olsson et al. | In-context Learning and Induction Heads | 2022
to: safe.interp
d: adv

== llm.behavior.memorization | Memorization & data extraction
def: Models can reproduce training text verbatim, raising privacy and copyright issues.
paper: Carlini et al. | Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models | 2020
to: safe.privacy
d: adv

######## L2: Model families
== llm.models | Model families
def: The major LLM lineages. Versions change monthly; families and their design choices change slowly. Dated, recent: re-check yearly.
pre: llm.basics
st: active
d: beg

== llm.models.bert | BERT & encoder models
def: Bidirectional encoders pretrained with masked language modelling (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, ModernBERT). Still used for classification, NER and embeddings, not for generation.
pre: dl.repr.masked
x: hist.timeline.fm.bert-gpt
paper: Devlin, Chang, Lee, Toutanova | BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding | 2018
yr: 2018

== llm.models.t5 | T5 & encoder–decoder models
def: Cast every NLP task as text-to-text with an encoder–decoder Transformer; widely used for fine-tuned seq2seq tasks.
paper: Raffel et al. | Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer | 2019
yr: 2019
st: classic

== llm.models.gpt | GPT family (OpenAI)
def: GPT-1 (2018), GPT-2 (2019), GPT-3 (2020), ChatGPT/GPT-3.5 (2022), GPT-4 (2023), GPT-4o (2024), the o-series reasoning models (from 2024), GPT-5 (2025). Defined the decoder-only scaling path.
x: hist.timeline.fm.gpt3
paper: Brown et al. | Language Models are Few-Shot Learners | 2020
yr: 2018
st: dominant

== llm.models.claude | Claude family (Anthropic)
def: Claude (2023) through the Claude 3 (2024), Claude 4 (2025) and later generations; known for long context, coding and agentic use, trained with Constitutional AI methods.
x: llm.post.cai
yr: 2023
st: dominant

== llm.models.gemini | Gemini family (Google)
def: Google DeepMind's natively multimodal models (from December 2023), successors to PaLM and LaMDA, with very long context windows.
yr: 2023
st: dominant

== llm.models.llama | LLaMA & open-weight models
def: Meta's LLaMA (2023) made strong open weights common; Mistral/Mixtral, Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma and others followed, letting anyone run and fine-tune capable models.
pre: llm.arch.recipe
paper: Touvron et al. | LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models | 2023
yr: 2023
st: dominant

== llm.models.deepseek | DeepSeek & efficient frontier training
def: DeepSeek-V3 (December 2024) and R1 (January 2025) showed frontier-level open models trained at low cost with MoE, multi-head latent attention, FP8 training and RL-based reasoning.
pre: llm.models.llama, llm.arch.moe
paper: DeepSeek-AI | DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report | 2024
yr: 2024
st: dominant
d: adv

== llm.models.embedding | Embedding models
def: Encoders trained contrastively to output one vector per text for search, clustering and RAG; compared on the MTEB benchmark.
pre: llm.models.bert, dom.nlp.sentence-emb
paper: Muennighoff, Tazi, Magne, Reimers | MTEB: Massive Text Embedding Benchmark | 2022
st: dominant
