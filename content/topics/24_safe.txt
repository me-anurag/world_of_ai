######## L2: Robustness
== safe.robust | Adversarial robustness
def: Keeping models correct under small malicious perturbations and unexpected inputs. Tiny, invisible changes can flip a classifier's answer.
pre: dl.basics.backprop, ml.concepts.shift
paper: Szegedy et al. | Intriguing Properties of Neural Networks | 2013

== safe.robust.attacks | Adversarial examples (FGSM, PGD)
def: Perturb the input in the direction that increases the loss, in one step (FGSM) or iteratively within a norm ball (PGD).
f: x' = x + ε·sign(∇ₓL)
pre: found.opt.constrained.projected
paper: Goodfellow, Shlens, Szegedy | Explaining and Harnessing Adversarial Examples | 2014

== safe.robust.training | Adversarial training & certified defences
def: Train on adversarial examples (the most reliable empirical defence) or prove robustness within a radius (randomized smoothing, verification).
pre: safe.robust.attacks
paper: Madry et al. | Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks | 2017

== safe.robust.ood | Out-of-distribution detection
def: Notice when an input is unlike the training data so the system can abstain or defer to a human.
pre: prob.bayesml.uncertainty
paper: Hendrycks, Gimpel | A Baseline for Detecting Misclassified and Out-of-Distribution Examples in Neural Networks | 2016

######## L2: Security of ML systems
== safe.security | Security of ML systems
def: Attacks on the ML lifecycle beyond test-time perturbations: poisoning training data, planting backdoors, stealing models, and exploiting AI-powered applications.
pre: safe.robust
paper: Biggio, Roli | Wild Patterns: Ten Years After the Rise of Adversarial Machine Learning | 2018

== safe.security.poisoning | Data poisoning & backdoors
def: Corrupt training data so the model learns hidden triggers or degraded behaviour; a growing risk with web-scale training data.
pre: infra.data
paper: Gu, Dolan-Gavitt, Garg | BadNets: Identifying Vulnerabilities in the Machine Learning Model Supply Chain | 2017

== safe.security.extraction | Model extraction & theft
def: Reconstruct a model's behaviour or weights by querying its API, or steal weights directly; a reason to secure frontier model weights.
paper: Tramèr et al. | Stealing Machine Learning Models via Prediction APIs | 2016

######## L2: LLM safety & misuse
== safe.llm | LLM safety & misuse
def: Risks specific to language models and agents: being tricked into harmful output, hijacked by injected instructions, or misused for scams, disinformation, cyberattacks or weapons uplift.
pre: llm.post.safety-tuning, safe.security

== safe.llm.jailbreak | Jailbreaks
def: Prompts that get a model to ignore its safety training (role-play, obfuscation, many-shot examples, automated adversarial suffixes).
paper: Zou et al. | Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models | 2023

== safe.llm.injection | Prompt injection
def: Instructions hidden in data the model reads (web pages, emails, documents, tool output) hijack it into following the attacker instead of the user. The central security problem for agents.
pre: sys.agents.tools
paper: Greshake et al. | Not What You've Signed Up For: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection | 2023

== safe.llm.redteam | Red-teaming
def: Deliberately attacking a model, by people or automated systems, to find harmful behaviours before release.
paper: Ganguli et al. | Red Teaming Language Models to Reduce Harms: Methods, Scaling Behaviors, and Lessons Learned | 2022

== safe.llm.misuse | Misuse risks (disinformation, cyber, bio/chem uplift)
def: Ways capable models could help bad actors scale scams and propaganda or gain dangerous technical capabilities; assessed with dangerous-capability evaluations.
to: safe.eval
d: beg

== safe.llm.provenance | Watermarking, provenance & deepfake detection
def: Mark AI-generated text, images and audio (statistical watermarks, signed metadata like C2PA) and detect synthetic media. Detection of unmarked content remains unreliable.
pre: llm.decode
paper: Kirchenbauer et al. | A Watermark for Large Language Models | 2023

######## L2: Privacy
== safe.privacy | Privacy
def: Protecting personal data used to train and query models: models can memorize and leak it, and inference can reveal sensitive attributes.
pre: ml.concepts.generalization, infra.data
paper: Shokri, Stronati, Song, Shmatikov | Membership Inference Attacks Against Machine Learning Models | 2017

== safe.privacy.attacks | Membership inference & reconstruction attacks
def: Determine whether a person's record was in the training set, or reconstruct training examples from a model.
pre: llm.behavior.memorization

== safe.privacy.dp | Differential privacy
def: A mathematical guarantee that any single person's data barely changes the output; achieved by adding calibrated noise. DP-SGD applies it to training.
f: P[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ e^ε · P[M(D') ∈ S]
pre: found.prob.dist.exponential
paper: Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, Smith | Calibrating Noise to Sensitivity in Private Data Analysis | 2006
st: foundational
d: adv

== safe.privacy.dpsgd | DP-SGD
def: Clip each example's gradient and add Gaussian noise during training, with privacy accounting across steps.
pre: safe.privacy.dp, found.opt.first.sgd
paper: Abadi et al. | Deep Learning with Differential Privacy | 2016
d: adv

== safe.privacy.pets | Privacy-enhancing technologies (federated, secure computation)
def: Federated learning, secure multi-party computation, homomorphic encryption and trusted hardware let models train or run without exposing raw data.
pre: ml.settings.federated
d: adv

== safe.privacy.unlearning | Machine unlearning
def: Remove the influence of specific data from a trained model without retraining from scratch; needed for deletion requests and copyright claims.
paper: Bourtoule et al. | Machine Unlearning | 2019
d: adv

######## L2: Fairness
== safe.fairness | Fairness
def: Preventing models from treating people unjustly by group (race, gender, caste, age, disability). Bias enters through data, labels, features, objectives and deployment.
pre: ml.eval.cls-metrics, found.stats.testing.sampling
paper: Barocas, Hardt, Narayanan | Fairness and Machine Learning: Limitations and Opportunities | 2019

== safe.fairness.sources | Sources of bias
def: Historical bias in data, unrepresentative sampling, biased labels, proxy features, feedback loops and mismatched deployment populations.
paper: Buolamwini, Gebru | Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification | 2018
d: beg

== safe.fairness.metrics | Fairness criteria (demographic parity, equalized odds, calibration)
def: Formal definitions: equal positive rates across groups, equal error rates, or equal calibration.
pre: ml.eval.calibration
paper: Hardt, Price, Srebro | Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning | 2016

== safe.fairness.impossibility | Impossibility results
def: When base rates differ across groups, calibration and equal error rates cannot all hold at once, so fairness requires value choices, not just maths.
pre: safe.fairness.metrics
paper: Kleinberg, Mullainathan, Raghavan | Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores | 2016
st: foundational
d: adv

== safe.fairness.mitigation | Bias mitigation (pre-, in-, post-processing)
def: Reweight or repair data, add fairness constraints to training, or adjust thresholds per group; plus auditing deployed systems.
pre: safe.fairness.metrics

######## L2: Interpretability
== safe.interp | Interpretability
def: Understanding what a model has learned and why it produces an output, to debug it, trust it, and detect dangerous behaviour.
pre: ml.interp, dl.basics
paper: Doshi-Velez, Kim | Towards A Rigorous Science of Interpretable Machine Learning | 2017

== safe.interp.attribution | Feature attribution & saliency (gradients, Integrated Gradients, Grad-CAM)
def: Highlight which input parts most influenced an output using gradients or path integrals; Grad-CAM produces heatmaps for CNNs. Useful but can be misleading.
pre: dl.basics.backprop
paper: Sundararajan, Taly, Yan | Axiomatic Attribution for Deep Networks | 2017

== safe.interp.concepts | Concept-based explanations (TCAV)
def: Test how much a human-defined concept (stripes, gender) influences a model's decisions using directions in its activation space.
pre: dl.repr.probing
paper: Kim et al. | Interpretability Beyond Feature Attribution: Quantitative Testing with Concept Activation Vectors (TCAV) | 2018
d: adv

== safe.interp.mech | Mechanistic interpretability
def: Reverse-engineer networks into human-understandable features and circuits, the way one would decompile a program.
pre: dl.arch.transformer.block
paper: Olah et al. | Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits | 2020
d: adv

== safe.interp.superposition | Features & superposition
def: Networks represent more features than they have neurons by overlapping them, which is why single neurons are often polysemantic.
pre: safe.interp.mech
paper: Elhage et al. | Toy Models of Superposition | 2022
d: adv

== safe.interp.sae | Sparse autoencoders & dictionary learning
def: Decompose activations into many sparse, more interpretable features using sparse autoencoders trained on a model's internals; scaled to frontier LLMs.
pre: safe.interp.superposition, dl.arch.autoencoder, ml.unsup.nmf
paper: Bricken et al. | Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning | 2023
d: adv

== safe.interp.circuits | Circuits, activation patching & attribution graphs
def: Find which components causally implement a behaviour by patching activations between runs and tracing feature-to-feature interactions.
pre: safe.interp.mech, prob.causal.do
paper: Wang et al. | Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small | 2022
d: adv

== safe.interp.steering | Representation engineering & steering
def: Find directions for concepts (honesty, refusal, sentiment) in activation space and add or remove them to control behaviour.
pre: safe.interp.mech
paper: Zou et al. | Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency | 2023
d: adv

######## L2: Alignment
== safe.alignment | Alignment
def: Making AI systems pursue what their developers and users actually intend, including in situations training didn't cover, and keeping that true as systems become more capable than their overseers.
pre: hist.define.four.beneficial, rl.reward.hacking, llm.post.rlhf
paper: Christian | The Alignment Problem: Machine Learning and Human Values | 2020

== safe.alignment.outer-inner | Outer vs inner alignment; goal misgeneralization
def: Outer: is the training objective what we want? Inner: does the trained model actually pursue that objective, or a proxy that coincided with it in training?
paper: Langosco et al. | Goal Misgeneralization in Deep Reinforcement Learning | 2021
d: adv

== safe.alignment.oversight | Scalable oversight (debate, amplification, weak-to-strong)
def: How humans can supervise systems smarter than themselves: let AIs debate, break tasks into checkable pieces, or study how weak supervisors can train strong models.
paper: Irving, Christiano, Amodei | AI Safety via Debate | 2018
d: adv

== safe.alignment.honesty | Honesty, deception & scheming
def: Whether models report what they believe, and whether they might strategically mislead overseers (e.g., behave differently when they think they are evaluated). Studied with model organisms and evaluations.
pre: llm.reasoning.faithful
paper: Greenblatt et al. | Alignment Faking in Large Language Models | 2024
d: adv

== safe.alignment.corrigibility | Corrigibility & control
def: Systems that accept correction and shutdown, plus "AI control": deployment safeguards that keep even a misaligned model from causing harm.
paper: Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, Armstrong | Corrigibility | 2015
d: adv

== safe.alignment.specs | Specifying values (constitutions, model specs)
def: Writing down the principles and priorities a model should follow, then training and evaluating against them.
pre: llm.post.cai

######## L2: Evaluations & frontier safety
== safe.eval | Safety evaluations & frontier safety policies
def: Testing models for dangerous capabilities (cyber, bio, autonomy, persuasion) and propensities before and after release, tied to company policies that require stronger safeguards as capabilities rise.
pre: llm.eval, safe.llm.redteam
paper: Shevlane et al. | Model Evaluation for Extreme Risks | 2023

== safe.eval.cards | System cards & transparency reports
def: Published documentation of a model's evaluations, risks and mitigations at release.
pre: infra.mlops.cards
d: beg

######## L2: Catastrophic & existential risk
== safe.xrisk | Catastrophic & existential risk
def: Arguments that advanced AI could cause irreversible global harm through misuse, accidents, races between developers, or loss of control over misaligned systems, and the debate over how likely that is.
pre: safe.alignment, hist.philosophy.future
paper: Bostrom | Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies | 2014
st: debate
d: beg

== safe.xrisk.orthogonality | Orthogonality thesis & instrumental convergence
aka: paperclip maximizer; power-seeking
def: Intelligence and final goals are independent; almost any goal favours acquiring resources, self-preservation and resisting shutdown, which makes misalignment dangerous.
paper: Omohundro | The Basic AI Drives | 2008
st: debate

== safe.xrisk.debate | The risk debate & expert statements
def: Leading researchers disagree sharply on the size of the risk; a 2023 statement signed by many AI scientists called extinction risk a global priority, while others see it as speculative.
paper: Bengio et al. | International AI Safety Report | 2025
st: debate
d: beg

######## L2: Ethics
== safe.ethics | AI ethics
def: The moral questions AI raises: who is harmed or helped, who is accountable, what should be automated, and what we owe each other (and possibly AI systems).
pre: hist.philosophy
x: hist.timeline.reality.weizenbaum
paper: Floridi, Cowls | A Unified Framework of Five Principles for AI in Society | 2019
d: beg

== safe.ethics.principles | Principles (beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, justice, explicability)
def: The recurring principles across hundreds of AI ethics guidelines, and the gap between stating them and implementing them.
d: beg

== safe.ethics.accountability | Accountability & transparency
def: Who is responsible when an AI system causes harm, and what must be disclosed: documentation, audits, explanations to affected people, contestability.
d: beg

== safe.ethics.labor | Work, economy & inequality
def: Automation's effects on jobs and wages, concentration of power and wealth in a few AI firms, and the distribution of AI's benefits.
paper: Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, Rock | GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the Labor Market Impact Potential of Large Language Models | 2023
st: debate
d: beg

== safe.ethics.surveillance | Surveillance & autonomy
def: Facial recognition, predictive policing and behavioural profiling threaten privacy and civil liberties.
pre: dom.cv.face
d: beg

== safe.ethics.weapons | Lethal autonomous weapons & military AI
def: Weapons that select and engage targets without meaningful human control; debated at the UN with calls for a treaty.
st: debate
d: beg

== safe.ethics.environment | Environmental impact
def: Energy, water and carbon costs of training and running large models, set against AI's potential climate benefits.
pre: infra.hw.energy
paper: Strubell, Ganesh, McCallum | Energy and Policy Considerations for Deep Learning in NLP | 2019
d: beg

== safe.ethics.creative | Copyright, creativity & attribution
def: Disputes over training on copyrighted work, ownership of AI outputs, and the effect on artists and writers.
pre: infra.data.licensing
st: debate
d: beg

== safe.ethics.wellbeing | Human–AI relationships & wellbeing
def: Over-reliance, emotional attachment to chatbots, manipulation and effects on learning and mental health.
pre: llm.behavior.sycophancy
st: debate
d: beg

== safe.ethics.moral-status | Moral status of AI
def: Could AI systems ever have experiences or interests that matter morally, and how should uncertainty about that be handled? Discussed under model welfare and robot rights.
pre: hist.philosophy.mind.consciousness
paper: Long et al. | Taking AI Welfare Seriously | 2024
st: debate
d: adv

######## L2: Governance & policy
== safe.governance | Governance & policy
def: Laws, standards, institutions and international agreements shaping how AI is built and used. Fast-moving: dates below are as of late 2026.
pre: safe.ethics
st: active
d: beg

== safe.governance.euaia | EU AI Act
def: Risk-based EU law in force since August 2024: banned practices from February 2025, general-purpose model obligations from August 2025; the 2026 AI Omnibus moved high-risk rules to December 2027 (Annex III) and August 2028 (Annex I).
yr: 2024
d: beg

== safe.governance.us | United States policy
def: Policy has shifted with administrations: Executive Order 14110 (October 2023) was revoked in January 2025 and replaced by a deregulatory order and a July 2025 AI Action Plan; NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (2023) remains a voluntary standard.
yr: 2023
d: beg

== safe.governance.global | International governance & safety institutes
def: AI safety summits (Bletchley 2023 onward), national AI safety/security institutes, OECD principles, UN processes, China's rules for generative AI, and standards like ISO/IEC 42001.
d: beg

== safe.governance.compute | Compute governance & export controls
def: Governing AI through its inputs: tracking large training runs, export controls on advanced chips, and know-your-customer rules for cloud compute.
pre: infra.hw
paper: Sastry et al. | Computing Power and the Governance of Artificial Intelligence | 2024
st: debate

== safe.governance.open-weights | Open vs closed model release
def: Open weights enable research, competition and local use but can't be recalled or have safeguards enforced; staged and structured access are middle paths.
pre: llm.models.llama
st: debate
d: beg
